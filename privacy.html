Privacy policy

Last updated: 19 September 2026

Tally is a personal expense tracker. This policy covers the Tally Android app. There is no public web service; if one is added, this policy will be updated to describe it before it goes live.

This policy is written from what the app's code actually does. It is not legal advice and has not been reviewed by a lawyer.

Who this is for

Tally is built for its owner's own use and is not distributed to the public. If you are reading this because you were invited to try it, the same terms apply to you.

What Tally collects, and why

To sign you in

If you choose "Continue with Google," Google shares your name, email address and profile picture with Tally's sign-in provider (Supabase Auth) so it can create or recognise your account. Tally never sees or stores your Google password, and never asks Google for anything beyond confirming who you are — no access to your Gmail, your contacts, or any other Google product — unless you separately and explicitly turn on Gmail-based auto-capture, described below.

The data you enter

Amounts, categories, merchants, payees and notes that you add, or that auto-capture reads from a bank notification or a bank's own email. This is the whole point of the app. It is stored in a Postgres database operated by Supabase, encrypted in transit and at rest by Supabase's infrastructure. Row-level security ties every row to your account: no other account can read or write it, including other Tally users if more than one person ever uses this deployment.

If you turn on Gmail auto-capture

Tally asks Google for read-only access to your Gmail (gmail.readonly) and requests only mail sent from a fixed list of bank, payment-provider and marketplace domains. That list is compiled into the app, and every message that comes back is checked against it a second time on your device before it is read at all.

Mail from any other sender is never fetched, never read and never stored. From a matching message Tally keeps only the amount, the merchant, the last four digits of a card, the transaction reference and time the bank stated, and a one-way fingerprint of the message used solely to recognise a repeat of the same alert. For a marketplace order confirmation it also keeps the order number, the seller and the line items, so a card charge can be matched to what it paid for. The subject line and the body of an email are never stored and never transmitted anywhere. You can revoke this access at any time from your Google Account permissions page.

Limited use of Google user data

Tally's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. Data obtained from Gmail is used only to provide and improve the expense-capture feature you turned on. It is not transferred to anyone else except as necessary to provide that feature, to comply with applicable law, or as part of a merger or acquisition with notice to you. It is not used for advertising, and it is not used to develop, improve or train generalised machine learning or artificial intelligence models. No human reads it.

What Tally does not do

  • It does not sell, rent, or share your data with any third party.
  • It does not show ads or use your data for advertising.
  • It does not use your data to train any machine-learning model.
  • It does not read any email outside the sender list above, even when Gmail auto-capture is on.
  • It does not track you across other apps or websites.

Where data lives

  • On your phone: a local database, which is the source of truth while you are offline.
  • In the cloud: Supabase (Postgres), so the same account sees the same data on more than one device.
  • Nowhere else. Optional agent features (automatic categorisation, anomaly detection) would send a merchant name and an amount to Anthropic's Claude API if that feature were enabled. As of the date above it is not enabled, so no data leaves Supabase for this purpose. If that changes, this policy changes first.

How long it is kept

Your entries are kept for as long as your account exists, because an expense tracker that forgets last year is not one. Deleted entries are marked deleted rather than erased immediately, so that a device which has been offline learns about the deletion when it reconnects; they are erased on request along with the rest of your account.

Deleting your data

Email the contact address below and ask for your account to be deleted. Your rows in Supabase and the account itself will be removed, and nothing is retained afterwards. Uninstalling the app removes the copy on your phone. Revoking the Google connection at myaccount.google.com/permissions stops future sign-ins and stops all mail access, but does not by itself delete data already stored in Supabase — ask, and it will be deleted.

Children

Tally is not directed at children under 13 and is not knowingly provided to them.
